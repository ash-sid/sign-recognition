    Eight of the 250 the model knows, picked because each has a large distinct trajectory rather than a handshape held in one place. The figure is how often that sign was read correctly on signers the model never trained on, so the last three are expected to miss sometimes -- when they do, the answer is usually still in the closest signs above.

    Sign square to the camera with your hands around shoulder height. Distance and position are normalized away; the angle of your shoulders is not, and height is measured against them rather than against your head. Signs needing both hands are recognised poorly: the non-dominant hand was rarely tracked in the training data, so the model barely saw one.

    Trained on 15 signers and tested on 3 it never saw, at 61.0% top-1 and 82.6% top-5 across 250 signs. Live accuracy will differ: the test signers all sign with the opposite hand to the training majority, and about a third of test sequences lose more than half the frames of the hand doing the work. Tracking quality is the largest single effect in the project, which is why it is on the panel above.
